FIRE Nate - About

One guy who loves spreadsheets. One guy who loves building web apps.

FireNate started as a personal spreadsheet for tracking a path to financial independence — the kind of thing one of us builds obsessively for fun. Turning that into something you can actually click around in a browser was the other's kind of fun. Together, that grew into a small set of focused calculators — because most of the math that actually matters for FIRE planning doesn't need a full financial-planning suite to answer.

Where the name came from

A short, mostly-a-joke video clip — with a surprising amount of truth behind it — about how this site ended up called "FireNate."

What this is

A collection of small, single-purpose calculators for the questions that come up again and again on the road to financial independence: how fast can I get there, what does a loan really cost, should the next dollar pay down debt or go into the market, what happens to my portfolio in a bad decade, and so on.

Every calculator is built to make a specific trade-off visible — not to replace judgment, and not to predict the future. They're ledgers: a place to see your own numbers clearly, not a guarantee of an outcome.

What this isn't

FireNate is not a licensed financial advisor, a broker, a bank, or a registered investment service, and it isn't affiliated with any financial institution. It doesn't manage money, place trades, or offer personalized advice. It's a set of calculators built by two guys — one who loves spreadsheets, one who loves web & app development — for anyone who finds them useful, on their own time and at their own risk.

Your data

There are no accounts, no logins, and no tracking. Everything you type into a calculator is saved only in your own browser's local storage, on your own device — it's never sent to a server, because there isn't one collecting it.

If you want to move your data — to another device, or to share a specific scenario with someone — the Profile Manager and each calculator's own "copy a shareable link" button let you do that explicitly, on your own terms. Nothing leaves your device unless you choose to export or share it yourself.
