Benefits & Contribution Limits

Max out what your employer already gave you.

The IRS sets a new ceiling on tax-advantaged accounts every year. Here's this year's numbers, broken into what they actually mean per paycheck — and how far you are from hitting them.

What this page covers

These are the employee contribution limits the IRS sets for 401(k)/403(b)/457(b) plans, HSAs, and IRAs — the same limits regardless of which specific employer or brokerage holds the account. Your own plan may have additional rules (vesting schedules, after-tax contribution options, employer match formulas) this page doesn't know about.

An HSA also requires you to be enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP) — this page assumes you already are, if you're using the HSA numbers. Not financial or tax advice; verify against your plan documents and irs.gov before making contribution decisions.
