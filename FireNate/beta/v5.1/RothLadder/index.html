FIRE Nate - Roth Conversion Ladder

Get your money out before 59½ — without the penalty.

Retire early and most of your wealth is probably locked in a Traditional 401(k) or IRA, where withdrawals before 59½ trigger a 10% penalty. A Roth conversion ladder is the standard workaround — this simulates it year by year, and shows exactly which account pays your bills each year.

Step 1 · Convert

Each year, move money from your Traditional account into a Roth IRA. You pay ordinary income tax on the conversion now — but no 10% penalty, since a conversion isn't a withdrawal.

Step 2 · Season 5 years

Each converted amount has its own 5-year clock. Once it matures, that principal — not the growth on top of it — can be withdrawn completely tax- and penalty-free.

Step 3 · Bridge the gap

Your very first conversion won't mature for 5 years, so something else — taxable savings, or money you directly contributed to a Roth — has to cover spending until the ladder catches up.

This models the conversion-ladder path specifically. A 72(t)/SEPP plan (a series of substantially-equal periodic payments taken directly from the Traditional account) is another common way to access funds early — it isn't modeled here, since it locks in a fixed payment schedule with its own strict IRS rules. "Available to withdraw" tracks converted principal dollar-for-dollar as a conservative simplification — real accounts may hold more, since growth on seasoned funds left in place keeps compounding.
