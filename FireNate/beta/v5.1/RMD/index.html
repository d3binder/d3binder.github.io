FIRE Nate - Required Minimum Distributions

The IRS eventually wants its cut.

Traditional 401(k)s and IRAs let you defer taxes for decades — but starting at age 73, the IRS forces you to withdraw (and pay tax on) a minimum slice of the balance every year, whether you need the cash or not. This projects those forced withdrawals using the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.

The rule

Starting at your RMD start age, you must withdraw last year's Dec 31 balance divided by a life-expectancy factor that shrinks every year — forcing a larger percentage out as you age.

It's taxable

Every dollar of an RMD from a traditional account counts as ordinary taxable income for that year — missing one, or taking too little, triggers an IRS penalty on the shortfall.

Why it matters for FIRE

A big traditional balance left alone for decades can produce surprisingly large forced withdrawals later in life — often a reason early retirees run Roth conversion ladders well before RMD age even kicks in.

Factors are from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table (used by most account owners). If your sole beneficiary is a spouse more than 10 years younger, the IRS Joint Life and Last Survivor Table applies instead and isn't modeled here. RMD start age is 73 for those reaching 72 after 2022, moving to 75 in 2033 — this is an estimate, not tax advice; confirm your own start age and factors with a tax professional or IRS Publication 590-B.
