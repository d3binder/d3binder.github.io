What this page covers

This models a standard taxable brokerage account — a regular investment account, not a retirement account. Tax applies only to the gains portion of what you sell, at whatever rate you enter (short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income; long-term gains usually get a lower rate).

If your money is in a different kind of account, the math changes a lot:

  • Traditional 401(k) / IRA — the entire withdrawal is taxed as ordinary income, not just the gains, plus a 10% early-withdrawal penalty if you're under 59½. Cashing out here is usually far more expensive than this tool models.
  • Roth 401(k) / IRA — your original contributions typically come out tax- and penalty-free anytime, but earnings can be taxed and penalized if withdrawn before age 59½ and before the account itself is 5 years old.
  • HSA — triple tax-advantaged, but a non-medical withdrawal before age 65 costs you ordinary income tax and a 20% penalty.

Federal taxes only — this doesn't account for state taxes, the Net Investment Income Tax, or the sale pushing you into a higher bracket for the year. Not financial or tax advice.
