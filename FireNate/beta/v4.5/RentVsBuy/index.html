FIRE Nate - Rent vs. Buy

Renting isn't throwing money away — it's a real financial choice.

Buying builds equity, but it also ties up your down payment and loads on taxes, insurance, and maintenance. Renting frees that cash to invest instead. This compares both paths side by side and shows which one actually leaves you wealthier, and when.

Buying

Your down payment and closing costs are spent up front. Each month you pay principal, interest, tax, insurance, and maintenance — but principal payments build equity, and the home itself may appreciate.

Renting

You skip the down payment and ongoing ownership costs. Whatever you would have spent on buying, instead, gets invested — the down payment on day one, plus any month where renting costs less than owning would have.

The comparison

At your chosen horizon, we compare net proceeds from selling the home (value minus what's still owed minus selling costs) against the renter's invested portfolio. Whichever is bigger is the financially stronger path — for these assumptions.

This models the pure financial comparison only — it doesn't account for the non-financial value of owning (stability, control over your space) or renting (flexibility, no maintenance calls), which are often just as important in the real decision. Property tax and maintenance are estimated as a percentage of the current, appreciating home value each year.
