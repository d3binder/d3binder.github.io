How this is modeled

  • Both scenarios start from the same monthly cash outlay: the mortgage principal & interest payment plus a flat escrow amount for taxes and insurance (an annual percentage of the original price, held constant).
  • Homes: the loan amortizes on a standard fixed schedule; home value compounds monthly at the annual appreciation rate ÷ 12. Equity = home value − remaining balance.
  • Stocks: any down payment is invested on day one, and the same monthly amount that would've gone to principal, interest, and escrow is invested instead, compounding monthly at the annual return rate ÷ 12.
  • If you mark the property as a rental, the expected monthly rental income offsets the carrying cost, so a smaller amount is treated as "sacrificed" in the stocks scenario. This is a simplification — it doesn't separately compound rental profit or account for vacancy, maintenance, or property management.
  • This is a simple model for comparison purposes, not financial advice — it ignores taxes on investment gains, mortgage interest deductions, transaction costs, and rent you'd otherwise pay if you didn't own.
