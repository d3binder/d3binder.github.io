FIRE Nate - Monte Carlo Simulator

History only happened one way — this tests thousands of others.

Safe Withdrawal Rate tests every real historical starting year. This goes further: it randomly reshuffles the same historical annual returns into thousands of alternate sequences your retirement could have lived through, to estimate a probability of success instead of a pass/fail count.

Bootstrap resampling

Each simulated retirement randomly draws a real S&P 500 annual return (1928–2025) for every year, with replacement — so the same historical years can repeat or skip in any order, not just the sequence they actually occurred in.

Sequence-of-returns risk

A crash early in retirement, while you're still withdrawing from a full balance, does far more damage than the same crash late in retirement. Shuffling the order is exactly how this risk gets tested.

Reading the results

The success rate is the share of simulated retirements that never ran out of money. The shaded band on the chart shows where most outcomes actually land — not just the median.

This resamples real historical years rather than assuming returns are perfectly random and independent (they aren't, in real markets), which is a common, reasonably grounded approach — but still just a model. Because it's random, the exact success rate will shift a little each time you change an input or click "Shuffle again"; that variation is expected, not a bug, and shrinks as you raise the number of simulations.
